Section 02
Why a Pre-Denial Dossier

Most prior-authorization denials for surgical procedures are issued on the basis of incomplete or generically submitted clinical information. The treating surgeon submits operative notes and chart summaries; the payer's utilization reviewer evaluates against a criteria pathway that requires evidence in a specific structure. When the required evidence exists in the record but is not organized in the way the reviewer evaluates it, the denial is issued not because the case lacks merit — but because the submission did not anticipate the criteria pathway.

The Pre-Denial Dossier maps the patient's clinical evidence to the anticipated criteria pathway before the payer makes the determination — so the reviewer's first look is at a complete, criteria-organized submission.

Section 03
Anticipated Payer Criteria Pathway

For lumbar spinal fusion, commercial payer criteria frameworks typically require documented evidence across four domains before authorizing the procedure:

Criteria DomainWhat the Reviewer Looks For
Failed conservative therapyDocumented trial of conservative measures (physical therapy, pain management, injections) over a specified duration — typically 6–12 weeks minimum — with documented failure to improve.
Structural pathology correlationImaging findings (MRI, CT) that correlate with the patient's symptoms and physical examination. The imaging must show a structural problem at the level proposed for surgery.
Functional impairmentDocumented functional limitations — not just pain reports — that affect activities of daily living, work capacity, or mobility, and that have failed to respond to conservative measures.
Surgical appropriatenessEvidence that the proposed surgical intervention addresses the documented pathology, that less invasive surgical options have been considered, and that the patient's overall health status supports the procedure.
Section 04
Clinical Evidence Mapped to Criteria

Failed conservative therapy — 14 months documented:

Physical therapy: Two separate courses (8 weeks each) at two different facilities, spanning 14 months total. PT discharge summary from the second course documents: "Patient demonstrates minimal functional improvement. Continues to report radicular symptoms limiting ambulation to less than one block. Recommends surgical consultation." Epidural steroid injections: Three fluoroscopy-guided L4–L5 transforaminal epidural injections over 10 months. Pain diary documents temporary relief (2–3 weeks per injection) with return to baseline radicular pain. Medications: Sequential trial of NSAIDs (naproxen 6 weeks, meloxicam 8 weeks), gabapentin (titrated to 1800mg/day, 12 weeks, discontinued for side effects), duloxetine (60mg/day, 8 weeks, insufficient relief).

Structural pathology correlation:

MRI lumbar spine, 3 months prior (Exhibit C — radiology report wording reproduced verbatim in the packet): grade II spondylolisthesis at L4–L5; bilateral foraminal stenosis at L4–L5 with left foraminal height reduced to 4 mm; disc desiccation and height loss at L4–L5 with a posterior annular tear.

Anatomy — reconcile before writing a single sentence of the submission

At the L4–L5 level, the exiting root within the foramen is L4. The L5 root is the traversing root at that level and is typically affected by lateral-recess or central compression, not by foraminal narrowing. Foraminal and lateral-recess compression are different findings with different root consequences, and the two descriptions are not interchangeable.

The earlier version of this dossier described foraminal pathology at L4–L5 and attributed it to the L5 root. On the imaging as worded, that pairing does not follow. Two possibilities have to be distinguished from the actual record and examination, not assumed:

  • The radicular syndrome is L4, consistent with the foraminal finding — in which case the clinical description must be corrected.
  • The radicular syndrome is L5 and is driven by lateral-recess or central stenosis at L4–L5, or by pathology at L5–S1 — in which case the imaging basis cited must be corrected, and the side and level re-checked.

This dossier does not resolve it. Verify the radiology wording, the side, the level, the examination findings and the planned procedure against each other before submission. A mismatch between the claimed root and the cited imaging is one of the fastest ways to lose reviewer confidence in an otherwise sound case, and a generic anatomy sentence must never be copied into a submission.

Functional impairment:

Oswestry Disability Index: 58% (severe disability). Ambulation limited to less than one block with assistive device. Unable to sit for more than 20 minutes. Unable to perform job functions (warehouse work requiring lifting, standing, and walking). On modified work duty for 8 months; employer has indicated inability to continue accommodations beyond 60 days.

Surgical appropriateness:

The spine surgery consultation records consideration of both decompression alone and decompression with fusion, and documents 4 mm of dynamic translation on flexion-extension films.

State the surgeon's reasoning; do not convert a threshold into a rule. Saying that decompression alone "is contraindicated" is stronger than this record establishes. Decompression without fusion is performed in selected patients with degenerative spondylolisthesis, and practice varies. What the submission should present is: the surgeon's documented rationale for adding fusion in this patient, the measured translation with the radiology report that reports it, the images themselves, and the specific wording of the plan's policy on instability. Let the measurement and the surgeon's reasoning do the work — an absolute claim invites a reviewer to produce the counter-examples.

Patient is 52, BMI 28, non-smoker, with no documented surgical contraindication.

Section 05
Pre-Empting Common Denial Rationales
Potential payer objection

"Conservative therapy was not exhausted — additional physical therapy or injection courses should be attempted."

Pre-emption

Patient completed 14 months of documented conservative therapy across multiple modalities. Two PT courses with two different providers, three epidural injections, and four medication trials — all documented with specific dates, durations, and outcomes. The criteria threshold is a reasonable trial, not indefinite repetition of failed interventions.

Potential payer objection

"Fusion is not indicated — decompression alone is less invasive."

Pre-emption

The surgeon's note addresses this directly and documents 4 mm of dynamic translation on flexion-extension imaging as the basis for adding fusion. Present that measurement, the images, the report and the surgeon's stated rationale, and map them to the instability provision in the plan's policy. Do not assert that decompression alone is contraindicated as a general matter — it is not, and the claim is unnecessary when the measurement is in the record.

Section 06
Documentation Gaps

Gap 1 — Flexion-extension films not included in prior-auth packet: The surgeon references dynamic instability on flexion-extension radiographs in the operative plan, but the images and radiology report were not included in the submitted prior-auth documentation. These must be attached — they are the specific evidence that differentiates fusion from decompression-alone.

Gap 2 — PT discharge summary from first course: Only the second PT discharge summary is in the submitted records. The first course documentation should be included to demonstrate the full 14-month conservative-therapy timeline.

Gap 3 — the anatomy question in Section 04 is unresolved. Until the radiology wording, the examination findings and the planned level are reconciled, it is not established that the imaging cited supports the radicular syndrome claimed.

Submission gap or clinical question? Not yet distinguishable

Gaps 1 and 2 are submission gaps: the evidence is described as existing and simply was not attached. Gap 3 is different. The flexion-extension films have not been reviewed here, and part of the treatment history is described rather than produced. Whether those turn out to be submission gaps or unresolved clinical questions can only be determined after the missing evidence is reviewed.

The earlier version of this dossier declared the case free of clinical gaps while the key films were outstanding. That conclusion was premature. This version does not make it.

Section 07
Submission Recommendation

Submit the dossier with the prior-authorization request. Attach: (1) this dossier, (2) lumbar MRI with radiology report, (3) flexion-extension radiographs with radiology report, (4) both PT discharge summaries, (5) injection records with dates and outcomes, (6) medication trial timeline, (7) Oswestry Disability Index scoring sheet, (8) surgeon's operative plan with decompression-vs-fusion rationale.

Resolve Gap 3 before submitting. Organising evidence around the anticipated criteria pathway means the reviewer sees a submission structured the way it will be evaluated. It does not make approval more likely in any measurable sense, and this dossier does not claim that it does. A submission containing an unreconciled anatomical inconsistency is worse than a late one.

Operational Note
Prevention Note

For future similar cases. A prior-authorisation checklist that maps required documentation to the criteria domains a reviewer applies would reduce the frequency of submissions that omit evidence already in the record. That is a workflow improvement worth making.

It does not prevent this class of denial, and should not be described that way. Denials also follow from genuine criteria disagreement, policy changes, and clinical questions a checklist cannot answer — including the anatomy question still open in this case. Nor was the clinical case "never in doubt": Gap 3 is unresolved as this dossier stands.

Forward-looking operational insight for the client's surgical scheduling and prior-auth teams.

Review Standard
Open questions and sources
  1. What does the radiology report say verbatim about the side, level and location of compression, and does it name a root?
  2. What do the examination findings show — motor, sensory and reflex — and which root do they localise to?
  3. At what level and on which side is the procedure planned, and does that match the answers above?
  4. What do the flexion-extension films and their report actually show, and how does the plan's policy word its instability provision?
  5. Is the first physical-therapy course documentation obtainable, and does it cover the period claimed?
  6. Which policy edition will be in force on the date of the determination?

Sources relied on.

  • The plan's spinal-surgery medical policy, in the edition in force at determination. Supplied by the client; governs over any general framework described here.
  • Standard lumbar root anatomy: at a given level the exiting root passes through the foramen while the traversing root lies in the lateral recess. Applied here only to identify an inconsistency, not to diagnose.
  • Fictional case exhibits. Imaging report wording is reproduced verbatim in Exhibit C precisely because paraphrase is where this class of error is introduced.
