Why the boundary is drawn here

Denial rationale, service line, payer, balance and a reported deadline are enough to decide what kind of work an account needs next. They are not enough to decide whether an appeal should succeed. That judgement needs the clinical record, the applicable plan policy and edition, the procedural history, the contract, and the account's collectability — none of which are in a no-PHI triage file. Every output below is a routing decision. None is a merits determination.

Section 05
Observed groupings in the rationale text

Three groupings are visible in the denial rationale text across the 47 accounts. These are observations about how the rationales are worded in this file. They are not findings that a payer misapplied its criteria, and they are not a basis for concluding that any account is appealable — that requires the record and the applicable policy.

Grouping 1 — post-acute rationales that reference acute resolution (11 accounts, 3 payers). Across SNF, IRF, LTACH and home-health accounts, the supplied rationale text repeatedly frames the decision around resolution or stability of the acute condition. Whether each account also addressed the separate question of continuing skilled need cannot be determined from the rationale text alone. If the client wants that assessed, these eleven accounts are efficient to review together because the same framework applies — but each still turns on its own record and its own plan policy.

Grouping 2 — retrospective status reclassification on cardiac and neurological presentations (6 accounts, 2 payers). The rationale text describes a reclassification made after the encounter. Assessing any of these requires the admission-decision timestamps, the documentation available at the decision point, and the applicable plan policy — none of which is in the triage file. No statement is made here about the strength of the prior submissions, which Clinovian has not seen.

Grouping 3 — post-payment severity adjustments (5 accounts, 1 payer). The rationale text describes removal or challenge of a secondary diagnosis after payment. These accounts cannot be assessed at all without the reported diagnosis codes and the grouper inputs, which the client holds. No payment differential is stated here: the difference between two DRGs is a contract calculation, not a property of the denial rationale, and the client's contracting team owns it.

Section 06
Suggested working sequence

A sequence the client may find efficient. Clinovian is not directing the client's appeal programme, and none of this overrides the client's own verified deadlines or its decisions about which accounts to pursue.

First: verify the seven client-reported deadlines against the notice dates and the applicable appeal process. Everything else is downstream of that.

Then: supply the missing items for the twelve accounts in the missing-information category. Several of these may reclassify once the information arrives, so resolving them early avoids re-work.

Then: decide which of the eighteen clinical-review accounts to commission, working down from the largest billed balance and taking the deadline-flagged accounts first within that order.

Alongside: route the ten administrative accounts to billing, coding or eligibility as appropriate. They do not need a physician.

Billed balances flagged for further review

$612,000 in the clinical-review category and $318,000 in the missing-information category, from a total file of $1,274,000. These are client-supplied billed balances — the amounts currently on the accounts. They are not a recovery estimate, not an expected value, and not an amount at stake. What any account would actually recover depends on the merits, the contract, the procedural posture and collectability, none of which this deliverable assesses.

Review Standard
Limits, open questions and method

What a reader should not take from this document. No account here has been assessed for merit. No denial has been characterised as correct or incorrect. No deadline has been verified. No payment has been forecast. Routing a denial rationale to a category is a statement about the rationale text and the information supplied, not about the underlying care.

Questions the client must answer before acting.

  1. Is each client-reported deadline correct against the notice date and the applicable appeal level and process?
  2. For the severity-adjustment accounts, what diagnosis codes were reported and what does the grouper return for the applicable version?
  3. For the missing-information accounts, can the absent items be produced, and does their arrival change the routing?
  4. Which accounts are worth the cost of review, given the client's own contract terms and collectability data?
  5. Are any accounts already resolved, adjusted or closed in the client's system but still open in the extract supplied?

Method. Each account was read on five supplied fields only: denial rationale text, service line or setting, payer, billed balance, and any client-reported deadline. Each was assigned to exactly one category on the face of that rationale. Deadline priority was recorded as a flag. Balances were summed once, by category. No record, policy document, contract or payer correspondence was requested, received or used.
