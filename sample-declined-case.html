Section 02
Decision
Declined — three independent reasons, any one sufficient

1. The deliverable requested requires a qualification the reviewer does not hold. The request was for a supporting statement attached to an external-review filing, where the identity and licensure of the author carry weight. Clinovian's reviewer is an MBBS physician who is not licensed in the United States and is not a psychiatrist. A clinical-reasoning memo for the client's internal use is one thing; a statement offered to an external reviewer as clinical support in an acute psychiatric case is a different instrument, and offering it would misrepresent what stands behind it.

2. Acute psychiatric continued stay sits at the edge of the competence claimed. Continued-stay determinations in acute psychiatry turn on current risk assessment, treatment response and discharge-planning feasibility — judgements a psychiatrist makes from a whole record, often with information that is not in the written chart. Clinovian reviews behavioural-health denials where the question is documentation and criteria mapping. This question is not that.

3. The timeline could not be met honestly. Six days, for a first engagement with no agreement, no approved record-transfer route and no procurement review, is not achievable. Accepting and then delivering late would have consumed the client's remaining window. Clinovian does not accept a first engagement on an urgent timeline (see the order specification, section 6).

What is deliberately not said. No view is offered on whether the denial was correct, whether the patient required continued inpatient care, or whether an appeal would succeed. Declining a case is not a judgement about it. A reader should not infer weakness in the client's position from the fact that Clinovian did not take the work.

Section 03
What the client received instead

The decline was sent within four hours of the fit-assessment inquiry, in writing, and contained the following. No fee was charged.

  • The reasoning above, stated plainly, so the client could judge it rather than take it on trust.
  • The two questions worth asking the plan immediately, given the deadline: which criteria set and edition was applied, and whether the plan had produced the reviewing clinician's specialty. Both are procedural, both are free, and both are useful whoever ends up writing the submission.
  • A description of the qualification the case needs — a psychiatrist able to attest in the applicable jurisdiction — so the client could direct its search rather than repeat it.
  • A note on what Clinovian could take on in that inventory: behavioural-health denials where the dispute is documentation and criteria mapping rather than acute risk assessment, on a normal timeline after onboarding.
Section 04
Why this specimen is published

A set of specimens in which every case is workable and every conclusion favourable tells a buyer very little. It does not show where the service stops, and a service whose limits are invisible is one whose limits are discovered later — usually at the client's expense.

The practical value of a physician reviewer who declines is that the cases which are accepted mean something. If nothing is turned away, acceptance is not a signal. The categories Clinovian declines are set out on the case suitability page; this specimen shows one of them happening.

On charging. A case declined before work starts is not charged. A case declined after review has begun, because the record does not support the analysis requested, is charged at half the agreed fee — and the client receives the written explanation of why, which is frequently the more useful output. That is stated here because a buyer should know it before ordering, not after.
